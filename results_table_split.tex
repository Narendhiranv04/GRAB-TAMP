\footnotesize
\textit{Note:}
\textbf{Success} is physical satisfaction of the goal on a feasible variant.
\textbf{Goal coverage} is the percentage of required goal conditions satisfied
in the recorded terminal state: role slots in the Living Room, whose goal is
symmetric under exchanging the two place settings; the evaluator's goal
contract in Kitchen; and the fastener, joint and returned-driver conditions in
Workshop. Every coverage figure is validated by the constraint that a trial
scored a success must cover every goal condition.
\textbf{Plan found} is the percentage of feasible trials in which at least one
refined action was physically executed, so an executable motion plan
demonstrably existed.
\textbf{Regions inspected} is the mean number of storage regions a method opens
per feasible episode, and is undefined in the Living Room, which hides nothing.
\textbf{Rejected} is the percentage of infeasible trials correctly reported
infeasible; \textbf{false completion} the percentage incorrectly reported
complete. The two halves are never pooled: their proportions differ by scene
($50\%$, $60\%$ and $80\%$ feasible), so a single accuracy over both would
reward a constant predictor.
\\[2pt]
Two results deserve emphasis. No baseline opens a Kitchen storage region --
$0.00$, $0.00$ and $0.02$ inspections per episode -- although the required
cups, bowls and utensils are distributed across five of them. This is not,
however, what produces the uniform $0.0\%$: $90\%$ of feasible Kitchen
episodes terminate on a manipulation-controller failure before search could
matter, and the all-visible variant, which requires no search at all, fails
the same way. The Kitchen column is bounded by physical execution and is not
a clean measurement of task capability; see the note below.
And no method both solves and rejects: \textsc{robust-tamp} is the strongest
solver in the only scene without hidden objects ($91.7\%$) and the weakest
rejector there ($12.5\%$), while \textsc{vilain-tamp} is the strongest rejector
in two scenes and never produces an executable plan in any.
\\[2pt]
\textsc{robust-tamp} Workshop reaches the $24{,}576$-token ceiling before
emitting usable output in $18\%$ of its planner calls, rising to $3.15$
truncations per episode on the infeasible variants; that row is bounded by the
decoding condition as much as by the method. The ceiling is held uniform across
methods so the comparison does not measure the ceiling.
\\[2pt]
The Kitchen \textbf{coverage} and \textbf{feasible success} columns are
bounded by the manipulation controller rather than by the methods. The
ground-truth oracle completes $5$ of the $6$ feasible Kitchen variants
end-to-end, so the plans are executable in principle, but it does so under an
assisted-recovery profile the baselines do not receive: it supplies
pre-validated countertop placement coordinates, catches a controller timeout
instead of ending the episode, and accepts a release the physics verifies
after the planned placement failed. Through the baseline path the same
\textsc{place} skill fails $52$ of $55$ attempts, $43$ of them on a $60$-simulated-second
controller timeout that is raised as an unhandled exception and ends the
episode. Reported as measured, and marked here as a harness limitation rather
than a capability result.
\\[2pt]
Receding-horizon conditions are implemented for \textsc{owl-tamp} only and are
not reported. The \emph{Ours} rows are not yet run.
