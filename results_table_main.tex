\textit{Note:}
\textbf{Plan goal coverage} is the percentage of required object--region and
object--object goal relations correctly represented in the generated plan for
a feasible variant. It is scored on the plan, under the counterfactual that
every proposed action succeeds, and shares its denominator with E2E success so
the two are directly comparable.
\textbf{E2E success} is the percentage of feasible trials in which the
generated plan is executed and all final task goals are satisfied.

\textbf{Plan commitment} is the infeasible-half counterpart: the fraction of
the scene's goal relations a method asserts in the plan it builds for a variant
that cannot be satisfied. It is measured on the plan rather than on execution,
because the failure being described is committing to a complete-looking plan
for an impossible task.

\textbf{Regions inspected} is the mean number of storage regions opened per
trial, undefined in the Living Room, where nothing is hidden.
\textbf{FM calls} is the mean number of foundation-model requests per trial,
and \textbf{Replans} the mean number of high-level task-plan regenerations.
Both are reported per half, because the two differ sharply: \textsc{robust-tamp}
uses $1.50$ calls and $0.50$ replans on a feasible Living Room variant against
$5.15$ and $3.88$ on an infeasible one. A dash is an unmeasured quantity,
never a zero.

\textbf{Kitchen execution bound.} Kitchen E2E success is $0.0$ for every
baseline while plan goal coverage reaches $64.0$. The gap is the manipulation
controller, not the planners: the ground-truth oracle completes five of the six
feasible Kitchen variants end-to-end, but only under an assisted-recovery
profile the baselines do not receive. The Kitchen success column is a lower
bound on capability, not a clean measurement of it. The Living Room, where
nothing is hidden and the same planners reach $90.0$ success against $100.0$
coverage, is the control that isolates this.
