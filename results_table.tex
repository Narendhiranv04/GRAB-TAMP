\footnotesize
\textit{Note:}
\textbf{Outcome correctness} is the percentage of trials with the correct final
feasible/infeasible outcome.
\textbf{Feasible-task success} is the percentage of feasible trials successfully
completed.
\textbf{Goal coverage} is the percentage of required task goals satisfied,
measured against the ground-truth goal decomposition on the recorded terminal
state. Living room is scored over role slots rather than object identity,
because its goal is symmetric under exchanging the two place settings; Kitchen
is scored against the evaluator's goal contract, which enumerates the required
pour, stir and serving conditions. Workshop is scored over the three
terminal conditions its instruction names: the fastener seated in the joint,
the joint fastened, and the driver returned to the workbench with an empty
gripper. Which driver performed the fastening is not checked against ground
truth, because the physical fastening skill already rejects an incompatible
driver. ViLaIn-TAMP records neither a terminal-state snapshot nor a
ground-truth decomposition in its artifact layout.
\textbf{False completion} is the percentage of infeasible trials incorrectly
reported as complete.
\textbf{Physical plan found} is the percentage of feasible trials in which at
least one refined action was physically executed, so an executable motion plan
demonstrably existed.
\textbf{Raw VLM requests} and \textbf{high-level replans} are per-trial means.
\\[2pt]
Every reported coverage figure is validated by the constraint that a trial the
harness scored a success must cover every goal condition; scenes failing that
check are withheld rather than estimated.
Kitchen's feasible half is taken from the re-run under the corrected
\textsc{pour}/\textsc{stir} admissibility harness, and its infeasible half from
the original grid, which needed no re-run because rejecting an infeasible
variant requires no pour. Kitchen feasible-task success is $0.0$ for both
model-driven baselines under the corrected harness: pours now execute and
commit, but neither method ever emits a \textsc{stir}, which the goal requires.
Cells marked \textit{re-running} are being re-collected after three harness
defects were corrected on 2026-09-09, and no value is reported for them. In
Workshop, method success had been scored on the physical joint state alone,
which is one clause of a three-clause instruction that also asks that reusable
equipment be left on the workbench; every episode that fastened ended holding
the driver, and because the executive stops as soon as its verifier passes,
the omission that was rewarded was also the omission it caused. In Kitchen,
\textsc{robust-tamp} read the gripper under a key its runtime does not
publish, so every placement, pour and stir after a successful pick was
rejected. In Workshop, \textsc{robust-tamp} additionally ran against a
serving configuration on which 67 of 100 episodes took a transport fault.
Receding-horizon conditions and the \emph{Ours} rows are not yet run.
